| **Project Name** | LeadRanking — Leadership Competency Evidence Ranking Assistant |
| **Domain** | Human resources development, leadership competency frameworks, information retrieval, explainable AI |
| **Problem Statement** | Public leadership frameworks describe competencies and behaviours relevant to leadership roles. However, a user may need to consult multiple frameworks and manually locate the descriptions that relate to a particular behavioural example. This project investigates whether an AI-assisted retrieval and ranking prototype can help users find relevant competency passages more efficiently while keeping the evidence and source visible. The tool supports competency exploration and development reflection only; it does not determine a person's leadership potential or make employment decisions. |
| **Evidence of the Problem** | Evidence 1: Public frameworks contain structured descriptions of leadership competencies and expected behaviours.<br>Source / citation: University of Minnesota Leadership Competency Framework — https://hr.umn.edu/Professional-Development/Supervisory-and-Leadership-Development/Leadership-Competencies-and-Assessments/Leadership-Competency-Framework; Government of Canada Key Leadership Competency Profile — https://www.canada.ca/en/treasury-board-secretariat/services/professional-development/key-leadership-competency-profile.html<br><br>Evidence 2: Published research studies leadership-potential assessment and succession planning, indicating leadership assessment is an established topic rather than an unexplored problem.<br>Source / citation: Groves (2025), "Assessing Leadership Potential for Succession Planning in the Digital Era of Work and Organizations," Academy of Management Proceedings. *(Final bibliographic record to be verified through the university library before submission.)*<br><br>**Evidence limitation:** these sources show that competency frameworks and leadership assessment exist; they do not prove every organisation experiences the same difficulty or that this proposed tool improves decisions. |

| **Engineering Gap / Limitation** | What practical or technical limitation remains unresolved?<br>Gap / Limitation: Ranking algorithms, leadership assessment approaches, and explainability methods are already studied separately. The engineering question for this project is whether a small, source-grounded prototype can rank relevant competency passages from multiple public frameworks, provide traceable evidence, and remain consistent enough for competency exploration. The gap is **to be investigated**, not a claim that no similar system exists. |
| **Initial Project Objectives (2–3)** | O1: To design an AI-assisted system that retrieves and ranks leadership competency passages from selected public frameworks using fictional behavioural evidence.<br>O2: To implement and compare a lexical retrieval baseline with a semantic-similarity approach, where feasible.<br>O3 (if applicable): To evaluate ranking relevance, source traceability, response time, wording consistency, and scope-control behaviour. |
| **Target Users** | Learning and development practitioners exploring leadership competencies; HR practitioners preparing non-decisional leadership development resources; individuals who want to explore public leadership competency frameworks. |
| **Data Sources (at least 2)** | Source 1: University of Minnesota Leadership Competency Framework — URL: https://hr.umn.edu/Professional-Development/Supervisory-and-Leadership-Development/Leadership-Competencies-and-Assessments/Leadership-Competency-Framework<br>Source 2: Government of Canada Key Leadership Competency Profile — URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/treasury-board-secretariat/services/professional-development/key-leadership-competency-profile.html<br>Source 3 (if any): U.S. Office of Personnel Management Executive Core Qualifications — URL: https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/senior-executive-service/executive-core-qualifications/<br>Supplementary: IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance dataset (public/synthetic, Kaggle) — used only to help construct realistic fictional behavioural-evidence test examples; not used to rank or score individuals. |
| **Similar to EduAdvisor** | Uses a defined set of public documents as its knowledge base; needs retrieval, source tracking, evaluation, and clear scope controls; must show evidence so users can verify outputs. |
| **Different from EduAdvisor** | Focuses on ranking competency passages relevant to fictional behavioural evidence, rather than answering academic-advising questions. Evaluation focuses on ranking relevance, traceability, response time, and wording consistency. It will not recommend an employment or promotion decision. |
| **Responsible AI Risks (at least 2)** | Risk 1: Misinterpretation / overreliance — a user may mistake a text similarity rank for a validated measure of leadership potential. Mitigation: label scores as relevance scores, show source text, and state limitations.<br>Risk 2: Bias and inconsistent ranking — wording, cultural context, or framework choice may affect which competencies are retrieved. Mitigation: use multiple public frameworks carefully, test equivalent paraphrases, and report differences.<br>Risk 3 (if any): Privacy — real employee examples may include sensitive information. Mitigation: use fictional, non-identifying profiles only. A fourth risk also applies: unfair employment use — outputs could be misused to rank employees or justify promotion decisions. Mitigation: exclude real-person comparisons and employment recommendations from the system scope. |
