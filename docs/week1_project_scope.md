| **Project Name** | LeadReady — AI Leadership Ranking & Competency Alignment Assistant |
| **Domain** | AI-Powered Talent Management / Human Resources (Succession Planning) |
| **Problem Statement** | Succession planning — identifying who is ready to step into leadership roles — is traditionally done through annual, manual review cycles based on manager opinion and informal reputation. This process is slow, inconsistent across departments, and prone to bias: employees who lead through influence rather than formal authority are often overlooked because they don't match a manager's default mental picture of "leadership material." Where organizations do attempt to rank candidates systematically, the result is typically a leaderboard or score with no stated justification — managers see *who* ranks higher, not *why*, which makes the ranking hard to trust, contest, or act on developmentally. Organizations need a way to compare employees against explicit role requirements and leadership competencies, with a clear, inspectable explanation behind each comparison. |
| **Evidence of the Problem** | Evidence 1: Only 32% of surveyed leaders expressed confidence that they have the right skills to lead transformation in their organizations, underscoring the gap between assumed and actual leadership readiness.<br>Source / citation: Korn Ferry, "Why AI-Readiness Is Vital to Your Leadership Succession Plan" — https://www.kornferry.com/insights/featured-topics/gen-ai-in-the-workplace-articles/why-ai-readiness-is-vital-to-your-succession-plan<br><br>Evidence 2: Organizations using continuous, AI-assisted succession tracking (instead of manual annual review) report up to 60% faster leadership transitions and more diverse succession pipelines.<br>Source / citation: 4Spot Consulting, "AI in Succession Planning: 9 Ways to Build Your Future Leadership Pipeline" — https://4spotconsulting.com/ai-powered-succession-planning-building-your-future-leadership-pipeline/ |
| **Initial Related Work (at least 3)** | Study / solution 1: Gloat Succession Management Agent — a commercial AI agent that scores candidate readiness across technical skill, leadership competency, organizational knowledge, and stakeholder relationships.<br>Source / citation: https://gloat.com/use-cases-agents/succession-management-agent/<br>Key limitation: Proprietary and closed; scoring logic is not visible to candidates or auditable by outside researchers.<br><br>Study / solution 2: "Reengineering Succession Pipelines in SAP SuccessFactors: An AI-Driven Framework for Ethical, Predictive, and Inclusive Leadership Readiness" — tests decision-tree and logistic-regression models for leadership readiness within SAP SuccessFactors.<br>Source / citation: Zenodo, 2025 — https://zenodo.org/records/17500957<br>Key limitation: Validated only on simulated/anonymized organizational data in a single enterprise platform; real-world generalizability and bias behaviour on authentic data are untested.<br><br>Study / solution 3: Phenom Succession Planning — uses skills ontologies and predictive analytics to surface successors, including less-visible "informal leaders."<br>Source / citation: https://www.phenom.com/blog/what-is-succession-planning<br>Key limitation: Skills-ontology matching can still under-represent employees whose leadership signals are behavioural/relational rather than skill-tagged. |
| **Engineering Gap / Limitation** | What practical or technical limitation remains unresolved?<br>Gap / Limitation: Existing AI succession tools are largely commercial, closed-source platforms embedded in enterprise HCM suites (SAP SuccessFactors, Gloat, Phenom). Their scoring logic is opaque to the employees being ranked, and even where a readiness score is shown, the *comparison against role requirements* is rarely surfaced in plain terms. There is a practical gap for a small-scale, explainable leadership-ranking approach that compares an employee's documented competencies against a defined role's requirements and states, in plain language, where the alignment is strong and where it's lacking. |
| **Initial Project Objectives (2–3)** | O1: To define a structured leadership competency/role-requirement reference to rank employees against.<br>O2: To build a ranking approach that compares each employee's documented competencies to the defined role requirements and generates a plain-language explanation of the alignment, not just a leaderboard position.<br>O3 (if applicable): To evaluate the ranking approach for demographic bias (e.g., across gender, age group) and document mitigation steps. |
| **Target Users** | Primary: HR / talent management teams and people managers conducting succession planning reviews.<br>Secondary: Students and researchers studying applied AI Engineering in HR/talent contexts. |

| **Similar to EduAdvisor** | Both projects follow the same AI Engineering lifecycle — a defined problem, a bounded data/document source, an AI component that transforms that source into decision-support output for a specific user group, and planned evaluation and monitoring stages. *(Based on the course's stated framing of EduAdvisor as a conversational FAQ-style assistant; confirm against the full EduAdvisor example.)* |
| **Different from EduAdvisor** | EduAdvisor is a conversational, free-text Q&A assistant that retrieves and answers questions from a document corpus (an information-retrieval problem). LeadReady is a structured, comparison-driven system that matches employee competencies against defined role requirements and outputs a ranked, explained comparison rather than conversational answers. LeadReady also carries materially higher Responsible AI stakes, since its output could influence real promotion and career decisions. |
| **Responsible AI Risks (at least 2)** | Risk 1: Bias and fairness — historical performance and promotion data can encode existing organizational bias (e.g., against informal leaders, specific demographics, or departments); a model trained on this data risks automating and scaling that bias rather than correcting it.<br>Risk 2: Privacy and confidentiality — readiness and performance data is sensitive personal/HR data; real organizational data must never be committed to a public repository, only public or synthetic datasets will be used for coursework.<br>Risk 3 (if any): Over-reliance / accountability — managers may over-trust a ranking without understanding its basis, leading to decisions made "because the AI said so" rather than with human judgment and accountability. |
